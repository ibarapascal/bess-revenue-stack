What five modelling shortcuts cost a grid battery’s revenue forecast

Open research  bess-revenue-stack
August 2026
50 MW / 100 MWh on GB half-hourly prices, March 2024 – January 2026 (672 execution days)
Four ordinary shortcuts inflate revenue by three to four times Degradation pricing dominates; the efficiency curve is innocent
The five results
1 Pricing wear at zero Implies 729 full cycles a year against the 346–451 a wear-aware schedule chooses overstates 42–78%
2 No state-of-charge headroom for reserve At £2/MW/h it commits 40.1 MW of reserve against 26.5 MW it could deliver overstates 3–15%
3 Assuming you can forecast A real forecast captures 60% of gross margin but only 48% of net — the gap widens as wear is priced higher costs 40–52%
4 One flat round-trip efficiency The two errors inside it have opposite signs and mostly cancel; what remains is the auxiliary load, not the curve −2.9% to +5.7%
5 One wear price for every service Peak-shifting ages a cell 1.8–1.9× faster than frequency response at equal throughput shifts net 5.0–7.6%
Findings 1, 2, 4 and 5 compare two arms under perfect foresight — an unreachable bound used because both arms share it Finding 3 is the one that measures what losing that foresight costs

Shortcut 3: a forecast gets 60% of gross but 48% of net The error lands on cycles you already paid wear for
Look here
Capture rate against perfect foresight, gross and net of the cost of wear
Unit: % of perfect foresight
Source: Makuhari Development analysis
  • Gradient boosting on lagged prices beats persistence on both measures, but the gross-to-net gap widens: 1.24× at the field-anchored wear price, 1.54× at the German one
  • The mechanism is not subtle. A mistimed cycle still consumes capacity, so the wear cost is paid whether or not the spread materialises
  • This is a claim about degradation-aware models, not about forecasting: at zero wear price the two capture rates coincide
  • Both predictors are deliberately naive — no weather, no outages, no fuel. A desk’s forecast should beat them, which makes 48% a floor

Shortcut 4: flat efficiency holds two errors of opposite sign With no thermal load it reads 2.9% LOW, not high
Overstatement against thermal load, and the level / shape split of the efficiency term
Unit: left %, right £k
Source: Makuhari Development analysis
The efficiency half is a level error, not a shape one: equalising the average leaves load dependence worth 0.7–1.6% of the term, at 1, 2 and 4 hours alike So the modelling effort belongs on where the auxiliaries are charged, not on resolving the converter curve — the opposite of where it usually goes

Shortcut 5: one wear price for every service costs 5–8% of net Peak-shifting ages a cell 1.8–1.9× faster than reserve duty
Effect of differentiating the wear price by service, at a £2/MW/h reserve price
Unit: left MW, right % against a single flat price
Source: Makuhari Development analysis; ageing ratio from module tests on 220 Ah LFP under grid duty profiles
  • Charging frequency response at the arbitrage wear price makes reserve look more expensive than it is, so the optimiser under-commits by 0.3–6.2 MW
  • Correcting it cuts cycling by 5.8–8.6% and raises net revenue by 5.0–7.6% — the same energy, priced by what it actually costs
  • It does not change whether the asset enters the reserve market at this wear price; at a higher one it would
  • The assumption underneath — damage proportional to throughput — is exactly what degradation physics is known to violate, and is flagged in the code rather than buried

Three modelling choices behind these numbers, and their effect
Where this differs from the way battery revenue is usually modelled
Source: repository documentation and the cited field measurements
ChoiceWhat it meansWhat it changes
Ageing as a power law,
charged at the margin		 Both ageing terms are power laws in their driver, with exponents below one, and only the cycle term enters the cost per MWh — calendar fade is a cost of ownership, not of trading Read as linear rates, the same model predicts 67% capacity loss over the field plant’s first three years against 4.1% measured. With the exponents applied, the field anchor is a 0.82 correction rather than a rescue
Auxiliaries separated
from the efficiency curve		 The converter’s load-independent loss is charged only when it runs; thermal management is modelled as power × time and swept over 0–0.2 MW Stops the same energy being charged twice, which is what makes the sign of finding 4 readable at all
No API keys,
no manual downloads		 The Elexon endpoints used need no key or registration; the client is self-contained and caches to parquet One command reproduces every number from nothing. No market data is redistributed here

How to read this, and where the data comes from
How to read this
  • This is a controlled experiment on one asset in one market, not a survey. The shortcuts are shown to be costly here, and shown to be common by citation
  • It is a study of modelling error, not investment advice, and not a revenue forecast for any asset
  • Reserve prices are a synthetic sweep. The wholesale strategy is indexed to the half-hourly reference price and excludes the day-ahead auction, where forecast error costs less
  • Every number is reproducible from the public repository, including the intermediate results and the verification script
